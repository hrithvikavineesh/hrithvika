Underwater Robots
The physics of underwater robots mainly comes from fluid mechanics, buoyancy, pressure, forces, and rotational motion.
The main forces are

1. Weight - W
   Acts downward.
   W = mg 
   It depends on the robot's mass.

2.Buoyant Force — Fb
 Acts upward.
 Fb = rho Vg 
 It is caused by water pressure being greater at the bottom of the robot than at the top.

 Buoyancy > weight → rises
 Buoyancy < weight → sinks
 Buoyancy = weight → stays at the same dept

3. Thrust — T
 Produced by the robot's propellers/thrusters.
 It can act in different directions to make the robot:

 Move forward/backward
 Move sideways
 Move up/down
 Rotate

4. Drag — Fd
 Acts opposite to the direction of motion.
 Fd = 0.5 rho Cd a v^2
 It is the resistance caused by water.
 The faster the robot moves, the greater the drag. 

5. Lift — Fl
 Acts roughly perpendicular to the water flow.
 It becomes particularly important for underwater robots with fins, wings, or hydrodynamic surfaces.

6. Torque — tau
 Torque isn't technically a force, but it's extremely important because it causes the robot to rotate.
 tau = rF 
 It controls:

 Roll → rotation left/right around the forward axis
 Pitch → nose moves up/down
 Yaw → robot turns left/right
